Why it matters

Many believers still read the Bible in a language that isn’t theirs.

Across India, many communities only have Scripture in a regional language — Hindi, Marathi, Odia — not in the mother tongue they pray and dream in. Without Scripture in their own tongue, languages weaken and disappear.

~60 Indian languages have vanished in the last 50 years. A whole generation could miss hearing God’s Word in the language closest to their hearts.

How a translation happens

Local believers translate. We train, gather and walk with them.

Each workshop: 5–7 days · about 30 people
25 local translators + 5 facilitators

  1. 1

    Gather a team

    20–25 people for the New Testament (30–35 for the Old) — elders strong in the mother tongue alongside youth.

  2. 2

    Train for a week

    An intensive workshop on translation method. Food, lodging and travel are covered so no one is kept away by cost.

  3. 3

    Draft and check

    Translators draft from gateway languages, self-check key terms, then check together in groups.

  4. 4

    Review every month

    Field staff meet each language group monthly until the text is ready to print and place in people’s hands.
